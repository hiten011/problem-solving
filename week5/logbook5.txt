Problem: [TomekPhone]

Design Thinking:
I needed to minimize keystrokes when assigning letters to keys. If the total number of slots was less than the number of letters, I returned -1. Otherwise, I sorted frequencies in descending order and assigned the most frequent letters first, cycling through keys while tracking how many presses each key already had.

Troubleshooting:
Initially, I missed multiplying frequencies by the press depth, which gave wrong results. Adding a counter (numKeys[idx]) fixed this by correctly accounting for how many times a key had been pressed.

Reflection:
Sorting and greedy allocation reduced the problem to O(M log M). I learned how careful ordering of tasks (highest frequency first) can greatly simplify weighted cost problems. TC: O(mlogm+m⋅n)


Problem: [HouseBuilding]

Design Thinking:
The task is to minimize the total cost of leveling buildings represented by digits. I stored the frequency of each digit (0–9) and then used a two-pointer approach. Starting from both ends, I repeatedly shifted the smaller frequency towards the middle, adding the cost along the way. This way, all values eventually converge to the same height.

Troubleshooting:
At first, I considered brute force by testing every possible target height and summing costs, but this would have been inefficient. The frequency compression with two pointers gave a more direct and efficient way to simulate the leveling process.

Reflection:
This solution runs in O(N × M) for processing the grid and then O(10) for balancing heights. I learned that when a problem involves “leveling” or “equalizing,” working with frequencies and merging from extremes can simplify the approach compared to brute force.
